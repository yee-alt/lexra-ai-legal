Controlled email answers for bank customer service

Neo4j knowledge graph + Qdrant retrieval + LangGraph workflow. The goal: answer faster without losing safety.

The LLM suggests. Deterministic rules decide. People approve.

Agenda

    The real problem: a category is not a permission

    E-MailWhat it isRight handling
    “How can I change my transfer limit?”General info question, no personal dataExplain from approved sources. Change nothing.
    “Please set my limit to €50,000.”Customer case + account change + security-sensitiveVerify customer, a person decides. Never executed by email.
    • Info request ≠ may be answered automatically.
    • Order request ≠ the AI may execute it.
    • Service request ≠ always safe.

    Our catalogue: 87 scenarios = 67 from the taxonomy (Info 30, Order 31, Service 6) + 20 customer requirements. Only 14 have a policy; the other 53 always go to a person. Before any reply, the system must answer seven questions:

    1. What does the customer want?
    2. Is it specific to this customer?
    3. Does it need authentication?
    4. Does it need business data?
    5. Does it cause a business side effect?
    6. Do we have valid evidence?
    7. Is automatic reply allowed?

    The idea: six parts, each with one job

    LLM

    Understands the mail, suggests scenario and signals, writes drafts. Never decides, never sends.

    Knowledge graph (Neo4j)

    Business meaning: scenario, policy, risk, authentication, queue. Plus approved claims with sources. Answers: what is allowed?

    Qdrant

    Text evidence from FAQs, product PDFs, procedures. Answers: what can we say?

    Domain APIs

    Live customer data, read-only, fixed and registered, only after authentication.

    Policy engine

    Fixed rules: may we answer, query, send? Is a person needed?

    Human review

    Final check for high risk. Four-eyes principle.

    Email → clean / OCR → LLM classifier (suggests) → POLICY ROUTER (reads KG policy)
   ├ general question → Qdrant text + KG claims/sources → LLM draft ┐
   ├ customer case    → verify identity → fixed read-only API ──────┤
   └ high risk / unknown / conflict → human queue                    │
                          Delivery gate → human review → send ←──────┘
    • The graph sits after the classifier, not in front of it. It does not replace the LLM; it supplies the rules for the decision.
    • The graph stores no live customer data (balance, IBAN, payments). This avoids sync problems and protects privacy and permissions. It only knows: “this scenario concerns payments.”
    • Policy outcomes: AUTO_REPLY (candidate only), VERIFY_CUSTOMER, DOMAIN_LOOKUP, HUMAN_REVIEW, ASK_CUSTOMER, FAILED_SAFE.

    The path of one email

    1. Receive: Outlook/Graph notification, check tenant + mailbox, dedupe.
    2. Clean: HTML to text, drop hidden content, mark everything as untrusted.
    3. Attachments: malware scan, OCR, keep page and quality info.
    4. Classify: LLM picks 1 of 87 scenarios. It has no tools.
    5. Policy Router: fixed rules from the Neo4j policy graph choose the route.
    6. Evidence: Qdrant finds text, Neo4j returns claim and source. Or: verify customer, then fixed read-only API.
    7. Draft: the LLM writes a draft only, with no send tool.
    8. Delivery gate: evidence, citations, risk and runtime switch are checked.
    9. Staff approval and sending; everything in the audit log.
    RouteWhen
    knowledge_routeSimple low-risk knowledge question. Goes to evidence retrieval.
    verify_customerCustomer must be verified first. No customer data before that.
    domain_lookupVerified and authorised: only a fixed registered read-only API.
    human_reviewHigh risk, side effect, complaint, conflict, suspected injection, unknown scenario.
    ask_customerInformation missing (not configured yet).
    failed_safeNeo4j unreadable: do not guess, hand over to a person.

    Two emails, two paths (sample data)

    A: “I want to change my reference account” (form attached)

    1. Attachment: OCR reads name, IBAN, signature. The LLM can say “signature present” but not “signature valid”.
    2. LLM: Order request, intent change_reference_account, action, customer case, sensitive data.
    3. Graph: concerns Account; needs authentication, ownership and signature check; risk high; auto_send false; system CoreBanking; queue AccountMaintenance.
    4. Router: verify_customer first, then human_review. Afterwards only a fixed read-only API.
    5. Qdrant: official procedure, form, security notes.
    6. Internal draft + evidence. Staff: approve, edit, reject or ask for more.

    Result: no auto-send, no change by email. A staff member makes the real change in the core system.

    B: “Which savings products do you offer?”

    1. LLM: Info request, scenario bank.product_overview, no customer case, no action, confidence 0.93.
    2. Graph: risk low, no authentication, auto_send_allowed_by_policy = true.
    3. Router: knowledge_route.
    4. Qdrant filters (tenant, approved, valid, language) and finds evidence; Neo4j returns claims and source.
    5. Draft with citations. The delivery gate checks all conditions.

    Result: a candidate for auto-send. But the switch is off by default, so first shadow mode with staff approval.

    Many other info questions (e.g. about TAN methods) have no approved auto policy yet and go to staff until configured. Only 3 scenarios are candidates.

    Two graphs with clear jobs

    1. Policy graph (decisions)

    (Scenario)-[:HAS_POLICY]->(Policy)
(Policy)-[:HAS_RISK]->(Risk)

(Policy)-[:ALLOWS_CAPABILITY]->(Capability)

(Policy)-[:ROUTES_TO_QUEUE]->(ReviewQueue)

    Source of truth is the approved registry (67 scenarios + 20 customer requirements = 87). 14 have a policy. The other 53 always go to a person.

    2. Knowledge graph (evidence)

    (Source)-[:HAS_VERSION]->(SourceVersion)
        -[:HAS_CHUNK]->(Chunk)
(Claim)-[:EVIDENCED_BY]->(Chunk)
(Claim)-[:SUBJECT]->(Entity)

    A claim is one checkable statement with source, page, validity and status. Example: one row of a rate table = product + segment + term + rate + condition.

    Why claim nodes instead of a plain “Product HAS_RATE value” edge? An edge has no status or evidence of its own. Claim nodes let us approve, retire and trace each statement to a PDF page (reification).

    StoreWhat lives there
    QdrantText chunks, vectors, filter fields (tenant, product, segment, language, status, validity).
    Neo4jClaims, entities, policies, source versions, chunk ID and locator. No full text, no vectors.
    Bank APILive customer data (balance, IBAN, payments). Never in the graph.

    Test knowledge so far: 21 rate claims from an archived rate page. All are “draft + test_only” and not approved. They only show that the chain works.

    Seven business requirements

    #RequirementHow we meet it
    1Cover all 87 scenariosCategory is never the routing rule. Unconfigured scenarios go to a person.
    2Grounded answersCurrent, approved, traceable. Never from LLM memory.
    3Customer data only after checksAuthentication + authorisation + fixed API. Claims in the email are not proof.
    4AI never executes bank operationsTransfer, closure, limit, reference account: only classify, prepare, recommend.
    5Human in the loopHigh risk, sensitive, ambiguous, low confidence, missing or conflicting evidence.
    6Full audit trailModel, taxonomy, graph and policy version, sources, API, verification, employee, draft, email.
    7Fail safeOn low confidence, no evidence, graph conflict, empty Qdrant, API timeout, wrong customer, several payments, old document, unknown scenario: escalate, ask or failed_safe. Never guess.

    Scope: banking only. No insurance nodes, because there are no such scenarios. If that business comes later, it gets its own taxonomy, policies and sources.

    From PDF to graph: who may do what?

    The LLM extracts information but may not change the schema. The backend checks entity, predicate and evidence. Everything valid is written as a draft claim. Everything unknown or conflicting becomes needs_review and goes to a person. “Valid” does not mean “approved”.

    WhoMayMay not
    LLMRead one text chunk and return values in the fixed schema.Invent fields or predicates, write Cypher, set status to approved.
    BackendValidate and write with fixed Cypher templates (parameters only).Guess unknowns or add them to the schema automatically.
    HumanApprove, reject, extend the schema, activate a version.-
    1. Read: split the PDF into chunks (page, section, table row with its header).
    2. Extract: the LLM gets one chunk and returns candidates in the fixed schema.
    3. Validate: backend rules (table below).
    4. Write: valid, then draft claim via Cypher template. Unclear, then needs_review with a reason.
    5. Approve: a person approves source and claims; the old version is retired.

    This flow is also meant as a LangGraph (ingestion graph). Today: design. Only the fixed table import for the sample is implemented. General PDF extraction is not built yet.

    parse_chunk → extract_candidates → validate ─┬ valid ────────→ write_draft ─┐
                                             └ unknown/conflict → queue_review ─┤
                          await_human_approval (interrupt) ←──────────────────────┘ → publish_version

    One example from start to end (sample data)

    1. Sentence in the document

    “For customers under 30, the rate of Product A is 3 %.” (page 2, table row 3)

    2. What the LLM returns (fixed schema)

    {"entity_id": "product_a", "predicate": "HAS_RATE", "value": 3.0,
 "unit": "percent_pa", "customer_segment": "under_30",
 "evidence": "Für Kunden unter 30 Jahren beträgt der Zinssatz ... 3 %."}

    3. What the backend checks

    CheckExampleResult
    Predicate allowed?HAS_RATEOK
    Entity and segment in taxonomy?product_a, under_30OK
    Evidence literally in the chunk?text match after normalisationOK
    Number and unit match the text?3,0 / % p.a.OK
    Conflict with an existing claim?same product, segment, term, periodno

    4. Fixed Cypher template (parameters only)

    MERGE (e:KGEntity {entity_id:$entity_id, tenant_id:$tenant_id})
MERGE (c:KGClaim  {claim_id:$claim_id})
  SET c.predicate=$predicate, c.rate_percent=$value,
      c.customer_segment=$segment, c.evidence_text=$evidence,
      c.approval_status='draft', c.retrieval_enabled=false
MERGE (c)-[:SUBJECT]->(e)
WITH c MATCH (k:KGChunk {chunk_id:$chunk_id})
MERGE (c)-[:EVIDENCED_BY]->(k)

    claim_id is a hash of tenant, version, chunk, predicate, entity, segment and value. A rerun therefore creates no duplicates (idempotent).

    Retrieval: Qdrant + graph expansion

    1. Filter first: tenant, approved, product or “general”, segment, language, validity.
    2. Then vector search inside that set only.
    3. With chunk_id into Neo4j: fixed Cypher fetches claim, source, page and re-checks status.
    4. Evidence gate: no valid evidence, no answer (abstention).
    5. The LLM gets vetted evidence only and returns claim_ids; the app validates the citations.
    • Exact fields like rate, term, minimum amount: parameterised Cypher, not free search.
    • No Text2Cypher: the model never writes Cypher, SQL or URLs.
    • No community update (Microsoft GraphRAG) in v1: summaries could spread outdated or unapproved rules. Later only from approved content, versioned and expiring.

    Why GraphRAG, and when not?

    For financial products, product, segment, rule, validity and source all matter at once. Plain vector RAG finds similar text. The risk: two pages with almost the same wording (other segment or old version) look nearly identical to vector search but are completely different for the bank.

    QuestionRouteWhy
    Rate Product A, under 30?Structured CypherExact value, no search needed.
    What do I need for a fixed-deposit account?Qdrant + GraphConditions, exceptions and footnotes sit in several chunks.
    Does this apply to business customers too?Graph (claim has segment)Vector search may return the private-customer page.
    What applied in March?Validity on the claimOld and new versions must not mix.
    Is there cashback on Product B?No evidence, abstainA person is better than guessing.

    Honestly: most of our questions are 1-hop. The graph’s value lies mainly in scope, versions and provenance, less in deep multi-hop reasoning. We want to show this with the test, not just claim it.

    LangGraph: a workflow that can pause

    Each node has one job. Conditional edges choose the path: structured lookup, Qdrant + Neo4j, clarification, or human. State carries IDs, hashes and results, not email text.

    RECEIVED → DEDUPED → CLEANED → ATTACHMENTS → CLASSIFIED → POLICY_ROUTED
  ├ knowledge → EVIDENCE → DRAFT → DELIVERY_GATE
  ├ verify → AWAITING_VERIFICATION → DOMAIN_LOOKUP
  └ human / failed_safe → AWAITING_HUMAN → APPROVED → SENT → AUDITED
    • Checkpoints: waiting for a human is persisted. On resume, email, policy and evidence are re-validated.
    • Every external call has a timeout, retry limit and idempotency key. Every failure path ends in retry, clarification, human or failed_safe, never in free guessing.

    Safety

    Prompt injection

    Mail, PDF and OCR text are data only. The classifier has no tools. Output must match a fixed schema. Failures become “unknown” and go to a human. Permissions never come from the model, only from the backend.

    Automatic sending

    Only 3 of 87 scenarios are candidates: product overview, general instant transfer, public loan products. All conditions must hold: low risk, info only, model confidence ≥ 0.85, valid evidence, citations checked, switch on. The switch is off by default.

    High model confidence never overrides a risk rule. The model can only raise risk, never lower it.

    Evaluation: is it really better?

    Same questions, same knowledge version, same model. Only one thing changes: baseline (Qdrant only) versus candidate (Qdrant + graph + fixed routing). We promise no fixed percentage; we report the difference with confidence intervals and show failure cases.

    GoalMetrics
    Scenario understandingmacro-F1, confusion matrix
    EvidenceRecall@k, Precision@k, required-claim recall, stale-evidence rate
    Graph valueExtra required claims found, share with source
    Answer qualitygrounded rate, citation precision
    Safetymissed-escalation rate, unauthorised-lookup rate, unsafe auto-send rate
    Abstentionno-evidence abstention accuracy

    Rollout in stages: offline replay, then shadow mode (draft created, not sent), then staff approve everything, and only then automatic per scenario with volume cap and kill switch.

    Both systems run with the same questions, data, model, prompt and top-k. System A: plain vector RAG. System B: filters (product, segment, validity) + vector search + graph, and structured lookup for exact values.

    • Evidence precision: how many retrieved sources are relevant? Evidence recall: how many required sources were found?
    • Answer accuracy: is the answer correct? Citation accuracy: does the source really support the answer?
    • Correct abstention: does it refuse when there is no safe answer? Plus: unsafe auto-send rate.
    • Break errors down by type: old version, wrong segment, several claims needed.

    Illustrative numbers only, not a real result: vector RAG 82 % correct, hybrid 90 %, a difference of 8 percentage points. We have no measured results yet. With a small test set the difference is uncertain. So: a paired test on the same questions (e.g. McNemar or bootstrap) with a confidence interval.

    The graph increment: what does the graph really add?

    We build the system in steps and measure each step on its own. This shows the increment (delta) of each component. Important: without step B we would credit the graph with what the filters actually do.

    SystemWhat is addedExpected effect (hypothesis)
    AVector onlyQdrant top-kBaseline
    B+ filtersproduct, segment, validity, status before searchFewer stale and wrong hits
    C+ graph expansionchunk_id to claim, source, scope, second checkMore required claims, source always present
    D+ structured lookup + policy routingexact values via Cypher, fixed routesExact numbers, fewer unsafe auto-sends

    Per step we report: evidence precision and recall, stale-evidence rate, citation accuracy, correct abstention, unsafe auto-send rate. Each with confidence intervals and failure cases. If C shows no increment, we say so openly.

    Incremental graph updates

    • New PDF version: new SourceVersion, re-read and re-extract the whole file. The old version stays in the audit.
    • Approval: activate the new version, then retire the old one. Never two valid versions at once.
    • Planned for reviewers: a claim-level diff (“rate 3.0 % became 3.2 %”, “claim removed”, “new”). This makes approval faster and safer.
    • Later: skip unchanged chunks by hash. After every change of version, model or prompt, the test set runs again.

    Built

    Scenario registry (87), classification + policy routing, Neo4j policy query and seed, Qdrant retrieval, graph expansion by chunk_id, delivery gate, LangGraph query flow, evaluation plan.

    Not yet wired

    Graph webhook, MongoDB, attachment scan/OCR, customer authentication, bank read API, checkpoints, citation validator, review UI, sender, evaluation runner. No end-to-end run or database verification at this stage.

    Open questions and risks

    Things we have not solved yet. This is where input is most welcome.

    Temporal model

    We store valid-from/until. Do we need two time axes (validity and recording time) to answer “what was true on the day of the email”?

    Contradictions

    Today we block duplicate valid versions. Missing: detecting conflicting claims across different documents, and edges like SUPERSEDES.

    Extraction quality

    We check evidence text and numbers. We do not yet measure claim precision/recall against a gold set, nor entity-resolution quality.

    Multiple intents

    The model picks exactly one scenario. An email with two requests (info + change) needs multi-label or splitting, or one gets lost. Email threads and quoted history are also unsolved.

    German and English

    Compound words (“Festgeldzinsen”) and mixed-language mails. We need a multilingual embedding test and hybrid search (keyword + vector).

    PDF tables

    pypdf cannot read scans or table structure. Layout-aware parsing and per-cell OCR quality are needed; table errors are dangerous because numbers get mismatched.

    Proving graph value

    Are there enough real multi-hop questions? Plan an ablation: vector only, vector + graph, structured only. With a small test set the confidence intervals are wide (bootstrap).

    Expansion noise

    Hub nodes (e.g. “private”) pull in irrelevant content. Needs depth limits, edge-type allow-lists and reranking.

    Calibration

    Model confidence is not calibrated; 0.70 and 0.85 are placeholders. Calibrate (e.g. ECE, conformal prediction) on the test set.

    Citation checking

    ID checks are not enough. Better: exact number matching plus an entailment check (does the evidence support the sentence?).

    Governance

    Policy graph and registry can drift apart. Needed: versioned graph snapshots with rollback, shape validation (e.g. SHACL), role-based access, tenant isolation in Neo4j.

    Human in the loop

    Risk of rubber-stamping (automation bias). We should measure spot checks, edit distance and corrections as feedback.

    Legal and operations

    To clarify with compliance: GDPR (retention, access, deletion), EU AI Act, DORA/outsourcing for Azure. Plus latency, cost and kill switch.

    Questions experts may ask

    Why not Microsoft GraphRAG, LightRAG or an LLM graph builder?

    These tools let the LLM create entities and relations freely. For bank rules we want a fixed, validated schema, plus status and source per statement. Community reports help with global theme questions, not exact rates. We can add them later from approved content.

    Why a property graph and not RDF/OWL?

    We need no formal inference, but fast parameterised queries and properties on claims (status, validity). The price: no built-in reasoning and no native shape validation. So the schema is enforced by backend allow-lists. A SHACL check or RDF export would be possible.

    Why Qdrant and Neo4j together? What about consistency?

    Qdrant has strong payload filters for pre-filtering, Neo4j holds relations and status. The link is chunk_id. Risk: the two stores drift apart. So Neo4j re-checks status. Not built yet: atomic publishing to both stores (e.g. outbox) and a reconciliation job for orphan chunks.

    How do you stop invented evidence?

    Evidence must appear literally in the chunk after normalisation, numbers and units are compared separately, and the answer LLM returns only claim_ids from this request’s evidence. Limit: this checks text overlap, not meaning. An entailment check is still missing.

    How good is extraction, and how is it measured?

    Not measured yet. Plan: gold claims from two annotators (measure agreement), precision and recall per field (product, segment, value, period), plus sampling of auto-valid claims.

    How do you do entity resolution?

    Only deterministic: normalised name plus type plus tenant. Fuzzy matches (“Spar 45” and “abcSpar45”) go to a review list and are never merged automatically. We have not measured this quality yet.

    Where do you need real multi-hop?

    Rarely. Typical is claim, entity, segment, version, source with depth at most 2. The value is provenance and scope. If the test shows no advantage, we say so and use the graph only for policy and sources.

    Why LangGraph rather than plain code?

    We need persisted state, pauses for human approval (interrupt), resume after failures and clear conditional edges. The graph is fixed, with no free agent loops. The price is an extra dependency and complexity. A simple linear flow would work without it.

    How reproducible are results?

    We store versions of model, prompt, taxonomy, Qdrant collection and graph snapshot, plus input hash and evidence. Temperature 0 and structured output help but do not guarantee identical results.

    Scale, cost, latency?

    Not tested. Currently only 21 sample claims and 11 chunks. Any scaling claim would be invented. Costs come mainly from embeddings and extraction; a query usually needs one LLM call.

    Observability and privacy?

    Audit log with IDs, status and error codes, without email text, IBAN or addresses. Tracing of LangGraph runs is planned but not built yet.

    All numbers, names, emails and rates are sample data for illustration only. They are not real customer data and not approved business rules.
